Core Concept

Workers' compensation is a no-fault insurance system that provides benefits to employees injured at work, regardless of who caused the accident. Employers fund the system; employees give up the right to sue. The Experience Modification factor (mod) adjusts premiums based on the company's actual loss history compared to industry average.

Workers' Compensation Basics

Definition & Key Features

  • A no-fault insurance system — employees receive benefits regardless of who caused the injury
  • Employer funds the system (through premiums); employees waive the right to sue employer
  • Covers work-related injuries AND occupational diseases
  • Mandatory in most US states and Bangladesh has equivalent provisions

Benefits Provided

Medical Treatment
All reasonable and necessary medical care for work-related injury/illness — hospital, surgery, medications
Rehabilitation
Physical therapy, vocational rehabilitation to return worker to productive employment
Lost Wages
Typically 2/3 of average weekly wage; starts after a waiting period (usually 3-7 days)
Death Benefits
Funeral expenses + ongoing payments to surviving dependents

What is Self-Insurance?

An employer that self-insures does NOT purchase a workers' comp policy. Instead, they set aside reserves to pay claims directly. Advantages: no insurer profit margin, more control over claims management. Requires state approval and proof of financial capacity. Some employers join group self-insurance pools to share risk.

Experience Modification (Mod)

What is the Experience Modification Factor?

The Experience Modification (Mod or EMR) compares a company's actual losses to what is expected for businesses of its type and size. It adjusts the insurance premium:

Mod = 1.00
Average loss history — pays the standard (manual) premium
Mod > 1.00
Worse than average loss history — pays a surcharge (more than manual premium)
Mod < 1.00
Better than average loss history — earns a discount (less than manual premium)

Mod is typically calculated using the last 3 years of loss data, excluding the most recent year. It is recalculated annually by the state workers' compensation rating bureau.

Work-Related Criteria

An injury/illness is work-related if an event or exposure in the work environment caused or contributed to it, or significantly aggravated a pre-existing condition.

First Aid Cases (NOT Recordable)

The following constitute first aid and are not recorded on OSHA 300:

  • Non-prescription medication at non-prescription strength
  • Tetanus immunizations
  • Cleaning, flushing, soaking surface wounds
  • Non-prescription wound closures (butterfly bandages)
  • Hot/cold therapy
  • Non-rigid means of support (elastic bandage)
  • Temporary immobilization (splint for transport)
  • Drilling of fingernail to relieve pressure
  • Eye patches
  • Removing foreign bodies from eye with irrigation or cotton swab
  • Removing splinters by simple means
  • Use of finger guards

Fatality Reporting

Employer must contact OSHA within 8 hours if a work-related fatality occurs OR if 3 or more employees are hospitalized as a result of a single incident.

Privacy Concern Cases

Do NOT enter the worker's name on the OSHA 300 log for the following types of cases: sexual assaults, mental illness, HIV/AIDS/hepatitis/TB, needle-stick injuries, and cases where the employee requests privacy. Use "privacy concern case" instead.

Annual Summary (Form 300A)

  • Summarize totals from OSHA 300 log
  • Post in a conspicuous place from February 1 through April 30 of the following year
  • Must be certified by a company executive (president, VP, etc.)
  • Retain forms for 5 years following the year they cover

🧮 Mathematics — Premium Calculation

Term Final Question (2015-16) — Pty Industries Ltd Workers' Comp Premium Problem

Formula 1: Manual Premium

Manual Premium = (Payroll ÷ 100) × Manual Rate
Payroll — Total wages/salaries paid to employees in the classification (in dollars). This is the exposure base for workers' comp.
÷ 100 — Workers' comp rates are expressed per $100 of payroll. Dividing by 100 converts the payroll to the right unit.
Manual Rate — The base insurance rate for the specific job classification code (SIC/NCCI code), expressed as $ per $100 of payroll. Higher-risk jobs have higher rates (e.g., roofing might be $30/$100; clerical might be $0.50/$100).
Manual Premium — The base premium before any modification. This is what an average company in this industry/size would pay.

Formula 2: Standard Premium (with Experience Mod)

Standard Premium = Manual Premium × Mod
= (Payroll ÷ 100) × Manual Rate × Mod
Mod (Experience Modification Rate) — A multiplier based on the company's actual claims history vs. expected claims for similar businesses. 1.00 = average; >1.00 = surcharge (more losses than expected); <1.00 = credit (fewer losses than expected).
Standard Premium — The final premium the company actually pays (before any other discounts/surcharges).
📌 Worked Example 1 — RP Trucking (from lecture slides)

Given: RP Trucking Company has $924,000 in annual payroll for Code 7229 (truck drivers). The manual rate is $13.71 per $100 of payroll.

1
Calculate Manual Premium
Manual Premium = (924,000 ÷ 100) × 13.71 = 9,240 × 13.71 = $126,879
2
Calculate Standard Premium for Mod = 0.83 (good safety record):
Standard Premium = $126,879 × 0.83 = $105,310
3
Calculate Standard Premium for Mod = 1.00 (average):
Standard Premium = $126,879 × 1.00 = $126,879
4
Calculate Standard Premium for Mod = 1.33 (poor safety record):
Standard Premium = $126,879 × 1.33 = $168,749
✅ A good safety record (mod = 0.83) saves RP Trucking $63,439/year compared to a poor safety record (mod = 1.33) — this is the direct financial incentive to prevent accidents.
📌 Worked Example 2 — Pty Industries Ltd (TF 2015-16)

Given: Pty Industries Ltd is renewing their policy in July. Last year they estimated $10,000,000 in wages; actual wages paid were $12,000,000. Current year they estimate $15,000,000 in wages. Manual rate = $1.858 per $100. Mod = 0.93.

Note: Workers' comp is calculated on current year estimated payroll (not last year's). The rate is then adjusted once actual payroll is known.

1
Calculate Manual Premium for current year (use $15,000,000 estimated wages):
Manual Premium = (15,000,000 ÷ 100) × 1.858 = 150,000 × 1.858 = $278,700
2
Apply the experience modification factor (mod = 0.93):
Standard Premium = $278,700 × 0.93 = $259,191
✅ Workers' compensation premium for the current year = $259,191
📌 Worked Example 3 — Delta Steel Ltd (Practice Problem 2.1)

Given: Delta Steel Ltd has 500 employees. Total payroll = $2,500,000. Manual rate = $5.20 per $100 of payroll. The company had an experience mod calculated as 1.15 due to above-average injury frequency.

1
Calculate Manual Premium:
Manual Premium = (2,500,000 ÷ 100) × 5.20 = 25,000 × 5.20 = $130,000
2
Apply mod factor (1.15 — worse than average):
Standard Premium = $130,000 × 1.15 = $149,500
✅ Standard Premium = $149,500. The 1.15 mod represents a $19,500 surcharge above the base premium — a direct cost of poor safety performance.

Quick Review

What is the formula for Workers' Comp Standard Premium?
(Payroll ÷ 100) × Manual Rate × Mod
What does Mod = 0.75 mean?
Company has 25% fewer losses than average → earns a 25% discount on premium. Reward for good safety.
What does Mod = 1.40 mean?
Company has 40% more losses than expected → pays a 40% surcharge above manual premium.
Why is WC called "no-fault" insurance?
Benefits are paid regardless of who caused the injury — the employee doesn't need to prove employer negligence, and the employer can't deny based on employee fault.
When must fatalities be reported to OSHA?
Within 8 hours of a work-related fatality or 3+ hospitalizations from a single incident.
When must Form 300A be posted?
February 1 through April 30 of the following year. Kept on file for 5 years.
What are the 4 main workers' comp benefits?
Medical treatment, rehabilitation, lost wages (typically 2/3 of weekly wage), death benefits
What is the advantage of self-insurance?
Avoids paying insurer profit margin; more control over claims management. Requires state approval and large financial reserves.
